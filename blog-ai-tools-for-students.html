AI Tools for Students: Free Options for Research & Writing

Reading papers, organizing notes, and cleaning up a draft eat more time than the actual thinking does. These free tools cut down on the busywork — no paid plan required.

None of these do the thinking for you, and none of them replace understanding your own sources — but they do remove a lot of the friction between "I have ten papers to get through" and "I understand what they actually say."

01 NotebookLM

Upload your readings, lecture slides, or notes, and ask questions directly against them. Every answer points back to the exact source, which makes it useful for studying without the risk of it inventing something you didn't actually read.

02 Semantic Scholar

A free academic search engine with AI-generated summaries of papers. Good for the early "is this source even relevant" stage before committing to a full read.

03 Consensus

Ask a research question in plain English and get an answer built from actual published studies, not a general web search. Useful for literature review starting points.

04 Elicit

Helps extract and summarize findings across multiple papers at once — handy when a reading list is longer than the time you have before it's due.

05 Humata

Upload a PDF and ask it questions directly, with answers cited back to the page. Works well for dense textbook chapters or long reports.

06 Grammarly

The free plan catches grammar, clarity, and tone issues in essays and reports — a quiet last pass before you submit anything.

07 ChatGPT (free tier)

Good for talking through an argument, outlining a structure, or getting unstuck on a first paragraph. Best used to think alongside, not to write the final draft for you.

08 Otter.ai

Record and transcribe lectures automatically, with a searchable transcript afterward — useful for going back to exactly what was said instead of relying on rushed handwritten notes.

A rule worth keeping: use these to understand your sources faster, not to skip reading them. Most instructors can tell the difference, and the understanding is the part that actually helps on the exam.
